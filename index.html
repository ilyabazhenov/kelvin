How it works

Set it up once.

Kelvin adds a microphone called “Kelvin Mic” to your Mac. Your call app listens to it, and Kelvin listens to your real mic.

  1. Install KelvinIt asks for your mic once and adds “Kelvin Mic” to the system.
  2. Pick “Kelvin Mic” in your call appIn Zoom, Meet or Teams audio settings. They remember it.
  3. TalkThe lamp lights up ON AIR while an app hears you. “How do I sound” plays back 6 seconds of you, before and after.
The Voice keys

Choose how you sound.

The same four keys as in the app. Noise stays on Normal; the keys change only the tone of your voice. Switch while it plays.

Already have noise removal?

What Kelvin adds.

macOS Voice Isolation and the filters inside call apps remove noise. Kelvin does that too, and then:

One setting for every app

Kelvin Mic is a regular system microphone. Anything that lets you pick a mic uses it, browser calls included, with the same sound everywhere.

It shapes your voice

Noise removal leaves a thin, boxy laptop voice. The Voice keys bring back warmth and clarity.

You can hear yourself

“How do I sound” records 6 seconds and plays them back before and after. The VU meter shows how loud others hear you.

It stays on your Mac

All processing runs locally. No account, and your voice never goes to a server.

Why “Kelvin”

Light has a temperature. So does a voice.

Light colour is measured in kelvins. An office ceiling panel is about 6500K: cold and flat. A lamp at home is 2700K, firelight about 1900K. The light on this page warms the same way as you scroll down.
